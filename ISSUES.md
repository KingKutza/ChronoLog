- (8.27) ENTAILMENT STATED BACK (engine build, 2026-08-27, standing unless Don objects): the projection algebra needed one rule the boolean ruling did not state, derived from "cross-frame projection only through staples": a PLACEMENT locates a fact and does not carry the object's other facts — an object on two calendars does not relate those calendars, so its calendar-A fact never draws on calendar B; reached through a STAPLE it is the opposite claim (the author connected them, "so we can project the two relative to each other"), so a stapled far object's placements DO count, one hop out. - (9.2, Don's question, and the answer that settles the weekend) "Three steps back — what is a 'weekend'? Is it a feature of the Gregorian calendar, or a cultural event that happens to be ubiquitous?" A CULTURAL EVENT, and the question kills both of the options that had it riding the calendar. The proof is that the Gregorian law and the weekend can be held apart without either losing anything: two people on the identical Gregorian law disagree about which positions are the weekend — Israel keeps Friday and Saturday, much of the Gulf keeps Friday and Saturday, Nepal keeps Saturday alone, the Christian West keeps Saturday and Sunday, and the two-day weekend itself is a twentieth-century industrial artifact roughly a century old on a cycle thousands of years older. A property of a calendar cannot be something two users of that same calendar answer differently. The seven-day WEEKDAY CYCLE is the calendar's — the law declares it, and the code already treats `weekday` as one of the law's own cycles. The weekend is a SELECTION OF POSITIONS in that cycle, asserted by someone. Same cycle, different assertion, and the assertion is not even uniform inside one culture: a nurse's weekend is not an office worker's, and a person may hold several at once (mine, the company's, the client's) and want to see them separately or together. So the weekend is an authored object over a cycle, held in the numbers a person can have many of — never a fact the law carries and never a default the app knows. CONSEQUENCES. (a) It does NOT ride the Gregorian law; attaching it there would encode one culture's assertion into the definition of a calendar, which is the trinity's exact prohibition — "any system that encodes a right way does in the same breath preclude other ways." (b) It is NOT seeded into a fresh document; `createEmptyWorkspaceDocument` keeps its ruling, "two structural frames and nothing else ... nothing renders or is written that the user did not put there", and the ~13 existing first-run assertions stand untouched. (c) What the app may do instead is OFFER it — a named thing a person adopts in one gesture, the way a calendar is adopted, which is a different act from the app believing it. An offer the person accepted is authored; a default they never saw is not. (d) THE GENERALIZATION, which is the real prize: a weekend is one instance of a class — a recurring cultural marking over a cycle position — and holidays, a sabbath, business hours, a shift rotation, an on-call week and a sprint cadence are the same object with different positions and a different cycle. Build the weekend as an authored cyclic group and all of those arrive with it, none of them as a feature. That also answers what the lens does: nothing. - (9.2, Don's question) "Calendar as synthesis of frames? If the 7-day week is a Roman frame, eras and leap days are Gregorian, and weekends are US industrial policy — can we have n frames apply n rules to make a composite that is our basis frame for a calendar? Or is this a can of worms hiding hydras inside?" One hydra, and it is findable, so the answer is yes with a discipline. The decomposition is already half-done and was not planned: eras are frames stapled together (ruled 8.19), and the weekend is an authored group over a cycle rather than anything the law carries (ruled today). What Don is asking is whether to finish it. THE CASE FOR: the layers he names really are independent, and the independence is arithmetic rather than aesthetic. The weekday cycle is incommensurate with the year — seven does not divide 365 or 366 — so the week floats over the month-and-leap structure and needs nothing from it but a single anchor: one staple saying "this day is a Monday", plus a period. That is exactly a frame with a cycle and an epoch, which the model already expresses. THE HYDRA: not every layer is that kind. Month lengths and intercalation are ONE system — a leap day changes how many days the year holds, which is the axis month boundaries sit on — so they cannot be two frames without an order of application, and an order of application is a precedence rule, and a precedence rule is an encoded right way, which the trinity forbids. So the discipline is a distinction between two kinds of contribution, and it is the whole answer: (a) A CYCLE CONTRIBUTION — a period, an epoch anchor, and names. Freely composable, ORDER-INDEPENDENT, any number of them: the Roman week, a market cycle, a tithe, a shift rotation, a lunar phase count. Two cycle frames can never conflict because neither reads the other; each answers "where in my own turn is this instant" from its own anchor. (b) A STRUCTURE CONTRIBUTION — levels, radices, intercalation, the ladder itself. Exactly ONE frame may own any given level, and an overlap is a REFUSAL stated in prose, never a merge and never last-wins. That keeps the trinity: nothing decides which of two claimants wins, because two claimants is a thing the person is told about and resolves. A composite is then total and deterministic by construction — every instant gets exactly one reading from each contributor — which is what projection needs and what a precedence rule would have quietly broken. FOUR CONSEQUENCES WORTH SAYING OUT LOUD. (1) A calendar becomes a pile of frames, and the "basis frame" is the composite — which means basis is already the right word, and "n staples = warp, Lines draws it" already describes how a composite is drawn. (2) Authored as n frames, EVALUATED as one: the composite must compile to a single law object once per document, memoized the way `CoordinateLaw` already is, not walked per coordinate read. "Functions paint, equations mean" — the n frames are the equation, the compiled law is the painting. Overscale demands it. (3) The genuinely hard case is not the Gregorian stack at all: it is a lunisolar calendar where the month depends on observation and the year's month-count depends on the moon — layers that reference each other, which is a fixed point rather than a stack. The model has precedent ("membership closure is the least fixed point, with provenance"), so it is solvable, but it is the reason the composite must not be specified as a pipeline. (4) It pays for itself immediately: a Roman-week frame plus a Gregorian-structure frame plus an era chain plus an authored weekend is not four features, it is one mechanism, and a person inventing an 8-day week on Gregorian months, or Gregorian dates with a Hebrew week, gets it without a line of new code. RECOMMENDATION: rule the two-kinds distinction now, build it after the 9.2 refinement lands. It is an architecture change to the substrate every surface reads, and it should not share a wave with the surface work.

- (9.2, Don, resolving the Labor Day report and ruling the law behind it) "There is no 1+ mark or legend. These are the only two events on that day, and the Code Freeze is set to fill, so it should be automatically treated as background." So it is mechanism (B), OCCLUSION, and admission is innocent — two marks in a cell with room for both. Confirmed cause: the zone wash is opaque. `app/lib/lens/zones.dart:86` builds the fill as `Color.lerp(theme.paper, color, 'zone.fill')`, and a lerp between two opaque colours is opaque, so a band laid over another object erases it instead of shading it. It looks like a wash only because the thing under it is usually paper. THE RULING, which is bigger than the bug: FILL IS GROUND, A MARK IS FIGURE. An object whose authored handling says fill is a GROUND and paints beneath everything; an object that is a mark is FIGURE and paints above. Draw order is therefore DERIVED FROM THE AUTHORED HANDLING and not from a layer field, a lens knob or a paint-order accident — nothing carries a z-number, nothing branches on which lens it is, and a person who wants an object behind the others says so by saying it fills. That is the trinity's shape: no enum, no special case, and the meaning is authored. FOUR THINGS FALL OUT OF IT AT ONCE, which is how a ruling earns its keep. (1) Don's bug is fixed by the order alone: Code Freeze fills, so it goes down first and Labor Day draws on top of it. (2) A GROUND SPANS BY NATURE, which answers the gap the lens slice flagged separately — "a zone does not span the cells it covers" — because spanning is what a ground does; it was only ever confined to a chip box because it was being treated as a mark. (3) A GROUND DOES NOT LANE. The one lane packer (`app/lib/lens/lanes.dart`) exists to keep FIGURES from colliding, and a ground has nothing to collide with, so zones stop needing a lane and the packer stops needing to know what a zone is. (4) THE WASH STILL NEEDS REAL ALPHA, for the case Don has not hit yet: two grounds over the same span. With honest alpha they stack and read as two; opaque, the lower one vanishes and the picture lies again. The standing rule holds either way — nothing disappears silently. Also worth noting for the lens wave: this makes "grounds, then figures" a single ordered pass every surface shares, rather than each painter choosing, and Lines, Spiral and Radial — which have no zone pass at all today — get one for free by joining that pass.
- (9.2, Don) "The start and end staple author duration bug is still not fixed." Correct, and the reason it is still open is worth stating plainly rather than as an excuse: the two tests that assert it are among the unconditional-`fail` stubs in `app/test/cards/card_refinement_test.dart` (9 of 9 in that file assert nothing), so no wave has ever been able to build against them. THE DIAGNOSIS, and both duration bugs live in the same twenty-four-line function. `_duration` in `app/lib/cards/object_card.dart:428` reads `event.duration?.coordinate.levels.firstOrNull` — the STORED magnitude and nothing else — falling back to `'0'` and `'minute'` when there is none. But a duration authored by two staples is DERIVED, not stored: the engine already computes it at `app/lib/core/staples.dart:1620`, `final derived = here.length >= 2 ? _derivedMagnitude(here[0], here[1]) : null`, and flags it on the extent as `derivedMagnitude: true`. So the engine knows the duration and the card never asks. Two consequences, and the second is the dangerous one. (1) An object whose start and end are both stapled shows a Duration row reading zero minutes — the row is not merely blank, it states a false magnitude. (2) TOUCHING THAT ROW MINTS A COMPETING TRUTH: `put` writes a stored `duration` magnitude, so a person correcting what looks like a wrong zero authors a second duration beside the two staples that already say it, and the object now carries a magnitude that can disagree with its own ends. That is a contest created by the surface rather than by the person. Fix direction: the row reads the EXTENT, not the record — derived when the staples say it, stored when a magnitude was authored, and it must show WHICH it is, because a derived duration is not editable in the same sense (editing it means moving an end, or replacing the derivation with an authored magnitude, and the person must be able to see which one they are doing). The `derivedMagnitude` flag exists for exactly that distinction and nothing reads it. THE SECOND BUG IN THE SAME FUNCTION, still unfixed and already ruled at entry (9.2) above: the unit menu's handler is `(name) => put(amount, name)` (`:450`), carrying the COUNT across verbatim, so 90 minutes becomes 90 hours instead of 1.5 hours. The conversion must go through the governing frame's own law (`unitsPer`) so a 23-hour day converts as that frame says. Both belong to one fix of one row, and the audit stands: every unit menu beside every magnitude — span, offset, falloff distance — carries the same defect until checked.
